Deep diveHow I designed it

Embracing the chaos

Emergent gameplay. Weapons, vehicles, crowds and NPCs all interact, so players create their own stories.

Immersion over complexity. A world that's fun to poke at beats a deep mechanic that needs explaining.

One unique feature. Randy the raccoon reacts to player actions, which gives it a lot of personality.

Learning from other games, for the right players

inspiration

Simpsons Hit & Run was the reference for the open-world sandbox feel, so I studied why it worked instead of copying it.

audience

I made design decisions based on the target audience: players who want to mess around, not master a system.

systems

Missions, NPCs, dialogue, a minimap and a physics-based car are all connected, so one action can ripple through the world.

Design challenge: introducing the world organically

What I ruled out. A voiceover or intro text would've been easy, but it broke a core pillar: let the player discover the world themselves.

What I did instead. NPCs explain the world through dialogue, so players pick things up as they play.

The trade-off. It meant heavy playtesting to make sure players didn't get lost or confused. It ended up being the right call.

Playtesting loop

Watch players no hints, no explanations
→

→

Looking backTakeaways

Don't fight the chaos

Giving systems room to collide produced better moments than scripting them.

Protect your pillars

If I hadn't written down the core mechanics, I would have lost sight of what made the game unique.

Test with real players

Playtests showed where players got lost, which is where my assumptions were wrong.
